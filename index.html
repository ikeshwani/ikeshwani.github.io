I study how the deepest layer of the ocean is built and kept in place. I run numerical experiments on GPUs, mostly with Oceananigans.jl in Julia, and analyze them with tools such as water mass transformation.

I advanced to candidacy and wrote a proposal for the three chapters of my thesis. Along the way I ran a grid convergence test to find the resolution needed to resolve the plume and its boundary layer, then scaled the simulations up to three dimensions and higher resolution on UCI's HPC3 and NCSA Delta.

Before oceanography I worked on binary stars at UCLA, led an undergraduate physics research group, and modeled a statistical mechanics system in Python.

Research

Antarctic Bottom Water forms when dense water sinks near Antarctica and spreads along the sea floor. Horizontal convection, a fluid driven only by heating and cooling at its top surface, is the simplest model of that process. My thesis starts there and adds ingredients one at a time: topography, time-varying surface forcing, wind and rotation.
